Fittel — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 May 2026

This policy explains what Fittel collects, why, where it goes, and what you can do about it. Fittel is a personal-trainer ↔ client coaching app: trainers prescribe workouts and review meal logs; clients track sessions and meals. We try to keep data collection to the minimum the product genuinely needs.

Who we are

Fittel is operated by Ting Teck Chuan ("we", "us"). You can reach us at tingteckchuan@gmail.com.

Summary, in one screen

  • We use your Google or Apple identity to sign you in. We never see or store your password.
  • Trainers create workouts and notes; clients track meals (including photos), workout sets, and link to a trainer via an invite code.
  • Photos and metadata live on Cloudflare R2 (object storage) and Supabase Postgres (database) under our accounts.
  • We send selected meal photos to OpenAI for nutrition estimation, and exercise photos for movement identification.
  • We use Firebase Cloud Messaging to send you reminders.
  • You can delete your account and all your data from inside the app at any time. The button is real — see Your rights below.

We do not collect: your contacts, browsing history, location, advertising identifiers, or biometric data. We don't run ad networks or attribution SDKs.

Where your data lives

  • Database (Supabase Postgres, AWS Asia-Pacific): account info, coaching content, meal log metadata, links between trainers and clients.
  • Object storage (Cloudflare R2): meal photos, profile photos, and any media you upload through the app. Access is via short-lived signed URLs; the bucket isn't world-readable.
  • Auth (Firebase Authentication, Google): identity records and OAuth/Apple sign-in tokens. Firebase issues the bearer tokens our backend trusts.
  • Push delivery (Firebase Cloud Messaging): the FCM device token you registered.

Third parties we send data to

  • Google Firebase (Authentication, Cloud Messaging): identity and push delivery. Firebase processes your account email, name, profile photo (from Google/Apple), and your device's FCM token. Firebase privacy.
  • Cloudflare (R2 storage, CDN): hosts media you upload. Cloudflare privacy.
  • OpenAI (nutrition + exercise vision): when you log a meal, the photo and any description you typed are sent to OpenAI's API to estimate kcal/protein/fat/carbs/fibre. When you use the camera-identify feature on the exercise picker, that photo is sent to estimate the exercise name. We use the OpenAI API (not ChatGPT) — under their terms, requests via the API are not used to train their models. OpenAI API data policy.
  • Supabase (managed Postgres): hosts the database. Supabase privacy.
  • Render (backend hosting): runs our API server. Render sees request metadata at the network layer.
  • ExerciseDB / AscendAPI (open-source exercise catalog at oss.exercisedb.dev): when the picker loads, your IP fetches their public catalog directly. Animated demos are served from their CDN. We don't send your account info to them.

We do not sell your data. We do not share your data with advertisers. We do not run analytics SDKs that profile you across apps.

Health data (Apple HealthKit)

If you grant HealthKit access, the iOS app reads steps, active energy, and exercise minutes for the days you view. We summarise those into daily totals and send only the totals to our backend so your trainer can see your activity. We never sell, share, or use HealthKit data for advertising. You can revoke the permission anytime in iOS Settings → Privacy → Health → Fittel.

Data your trainer can see

If you're a client linked to a trainer (via invite code), that trainer can see: your profile (name, photo, goals), your workouts and how you completed each set, your meal photos and AI macro estimates, your off-days, and your scheduled sessions. They cannot see your raw HealthKit values directly — only the daily summary.

If you delete your account, your trainer's view of you disappears. If your trainer deletes their account, you keep your meal logs and personal data; their prescribed workouts and diet notes remain on your profile but are unattributed to any trainer (so your training history isn't lost).

How long we keep your data

  • While your account exists: indefinitely, until you delete it.
  • After account deletion: domain rows are removed immediately. Backups roll off within 30 days.
  • Server logs: request error logs roll off within 30 days. They contain user IDs but no PII bodies.
  • OpenAI API: per their policy, request data is retained for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring then deleted.

Your rights

You can, at any time:

  • View your data — it's all visible inside the app.
  • Delete your account in-app: open Profile → Delete Account → confirm. This removes your account, your media, and your linked records, and deletes your Firebase auth identity. The button is functional, not a stub.
  • Email us at tingteckchuan@gmail.com for any other request — including a copy of your data, correction, or to ask a question.

Depending on where you live (e.g. EU/UK under GDPR, California under CCPA, or similar laws elsewhere), you may have additional rights such as data portability, restriction of processing, and the right to lodge a complaint with your local data-protection authority. We honour these — email us.

International transfers

Our processors store data outside your home country (Supabase in AWS Asia-Pacific, Cloudflare globally, Firebase in the United States, OpenAI in the United States). Where required, transfers rely on Standard Contractual Clauses or equivalent safeguards.

Children

Fittel is not intended for users under 13. If we discover we hold data from a child under 13, we will delete it. If you're a parent who believes we hold data about your child, email us and we'll act promptly.

Security

Connections to our backend use HTTPS. Auth tokens are short-lived Firebase JWTs. Storage URLs for media are presigned and time-limited. We don't claim perfect security — no service can — but we treat this seriously and limit blast radius where we can.

Changes to this policy

We'll update this page when something materially changes (a new processor, a new data category, a new region). The "Last updated" date at the top reflects the latest revision. For significant changes, we'll surface a notice in-app on first launch.
